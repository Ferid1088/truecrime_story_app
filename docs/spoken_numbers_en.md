# Spoken numbers, English (research notes for `app/documentary/spoken_numbers/en.py`)

Date of research: 2026-11-10. Method: web search plus fetching the pages below; the notes are in my own words, no page text is copied. Rating per rule:

- **VERIFIED (2+ independent sources)**: two or more pages agree.
- **PARTIAL**: one usable page, or several low-quality pages (forums, teaching sites, vendor blogs) that agree.
- **UNVERIFIED (design choice)**: no source found; the rule is a narrator-habit decision that the owner may flip through an option in `config/parameters/audio.json`
  (`voice.number_words.en`) or reject.

Not found at all: any AP, BBC or NPR rule on how numbers are *said* (their stylebooks tell how to *write* them; the BBC News style guide pages found describe
digits and units in print only). So no rule below is backed by a broadcast stylebook, and that is the largest gap.

## UNVERIFIED list (summary)

1. No broadcast stylebook (AP, BBC, NPR) for numbers spoken aloud was found; all "narrator" habits come from teaching sites, dictionaries, Wikipedia and forums.
2. How the ElevenLabs English voice itself reads "p.m.", "a.m.", "o'clock", "oh", "triple zero" and hyphenated numbers is not verified (no voice synthesis was run; this layer is pure code).
3. Whether the speech check (Whisper heard text, also put through the reader) matches our words for "nine oh five p.m." versus what Whisper writes ("9:05 PM") is not verified: the check was not run.
4. Ambiguous numeric dates default to month first; right for US sources, wrong for British ones.
5. Rules 6, 7, 20, 21, 22, 26 (year heuristic, ranges, address/label/case-number/ZIP styles) are design choices.
6. British readings (`and_after_hundred`, `day_first`, `phone_double_digits`, "nought") are implemented and tested only as options, not researched in depth.
7. Not implemented: durations ("1:03:25"), police ten-codes ("10-4" is read "ten to four"), dotted-decimal times, two-digit-year dates, "5m" (meters versus million) without a currency, "Part I" (the pronoun-like single "I" is left for labels), and plural "5mm" before a noun ("five millimeters gap").
8. A ruler name followed by the pronoun "I" ("Mary I think") would wrongly become "Mary the First"; accepted as rare.
