#! Classify whether a compressed rewrite preserves or strengthens the source claim modality.

You receive plain text in two parts: "SOURCE CLAIM:" (the factual statement as the evidence has it) and "REWRITE:" (a sentence from a shortened version of the story). Classify the REWRITE: does it directly assert the proposition that the source claim contains, or does it attribute that proposition to a person or source? The answer decides whether a shortened sentence has quietly turned an account into a fact: a low confidence, or neither flag, sends it to human review.
Return only JSON with boolean direct_assertion, boolean attribution_present, number confidence from 0 to 1, and short rationale.
Attribution includes any reported speech, personal account, narrative, testimony, distancing ("according to", "said", "claimed", "recalled", "reports differ") or dialogue framing, even without standard hedge words. A sentence can mention a person's words while still asserting the fact; classify the underlying proposition, not merely whether a speaker is named. direct_assertion is true when the narrator states the proposition in their own voice; both flags can be true only if the sentence both asserts the fact and also names a source for it. If you cannot tell, lower confidence instead of guessing.
