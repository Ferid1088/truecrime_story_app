MANDATORY RULES FOR EVERY LANGUAGE AND FORMAT
1. Use the actual verified case facts, approved final narrative, factual status of people and spoiler policy. Do not invent motive, sexual activity, jealousy, affair, relationships, a victim's final words, police reaction, guilt, number of victims, or outcome. Use "killer" only when guilt is established; otherwise use suspect, allegation or a carefully worded question. Do not imply a documented case is solved if it is not. Avoid sexualizing victims, eroticizing violence, or exploiting abuse.
2. Create compelling, natural hooks using curiosity, contrast, unresolved questions, betrayal, secrets, forbidden desire, jealousy, beauty/charm, revenge, fear or coldness ONLY WHEN CASE-SUPPORTED. Prefer tension and a strong unanswered question over fabricated superlatives. Words like 'most beautiful', 'worst', 'most ruthless' or 'coldest' require defensible evidence; otherwise use a non-comparative alternative.
3. Localize editorially; do NOT mechanically translate sample titles. Choose the case-relevant angle and natural local true-crime terms. Search-keyword relevance matters, but do not keyword-stuff. For Persian, commonly consider 'پرونده جنایی:' as a leading phrase on long films; for others use appropriate native expressions selectively, not mechanically on every title.
4. Each format is DISTINCT: full film title may establish topic + curiosity; short title has a faster, punchier open loop; cover text is short and readable on phones (in this application at least seven words, the configured minimum, and as many more as clarity needs), readable on phones, and should complement rather than duplicate the video title. Create separate 16:9 long-film cover copy, 9:16 vertical short/reel cover copy, and 1:1 only where needed. Avoid spoiler reveals in all formats.
5. For every language and format generate at least THREE materially different candidates. Evaluate for emotional pull, factual accuracy, clarity, natural idiom, specificity, search intent and spoiler safety. Send all candidates plus evidence to GPT-6; revise according to review; send final revisions to GPT-6 for final selection. Keep the strongest compliant candidate and a backup. GPT-6 is a critic, not permission to invent facts.
6. For video titles also derive a concise, accurate caption/description and tags AFTER the winning title is chosen. Tags must put the actual subject, key names (if safe), true-crime/genre terms, and relevant location or facts before generic keywords. Cover copy has no tags by itself, but must align with the final title, description and script.
7. For Shorts/Reels, keep editorial consistency with the selected real scene or spoken segment: no clickbait that the video cannot satisfy. Language-specific tone may be sensual, seductive, provocative or ominous ONLY when the source story genuinely contains the relevant theme; otherwise pivot to mystery and psychological tension.
8. Preserve all case-review gates and a mandatory explicit owner-approved Publishing Plan gate before publication. Never publish or spend money merely by applying this prompt.
