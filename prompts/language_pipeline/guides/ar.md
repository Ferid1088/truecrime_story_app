LANGUAGE GUIDE: ARABIC (ar)
VOICE: clear Modern Standard Arabic of a good documentary narrator, accessible and spoken-friendly ("فصحى مبسطة"), neither academic nor dialect. Dialect only inside a quotation whose supporting_text is dialect.
SENTENCES: let the verb come first where Arabic does it naturally (not English subject-first order); medium sentences; parallel structure may give rhythm but never rhymed prose (no سجع padding). Avoid long chains of "و" and "حيث إن / في حين أن"; split them. Prefer active verbs over "تم + مصدر" and "قام بـ + مصدر".
WORDS TO AVOID (calques): "يعتبر" for "is considered", "تم القبض/الاستجواب" as a default passive, literal translations of English idioms, "الجدير بالذكر", "في الواقع" as filler.
CULTURE: tell family and household relations with the precise terms (زوجة الأب، ابنة الزوج، أخت غير شقيقة) and with the restraint the audience expects around death and family; keep violent detail to what the evidence carries and tell it plainly. Explain foreign institutions by function in one clause (هيئة المحلفين، المصرف، البلدة). No religious, moral or political framing that the evidence does not contain; no comparison to a local case or place that the evidence does not contain.
NAMES AND NUMBERS: transliterate foreign names with the usual Arabic spelling and keep it identical throughout (full name first, then the short name). Gregorian dates "4 أغسطس 1892" with one month-naming convention throughout; Western digits 0-9 throughout; amounts in dollars as stated; distances by plain conversion with "نحو". Punctuation: Arabic comma "،" and question mark "؟".
CHECK: read each paragraph aloud in your head. If the word order or the connectors sound like English, re-tell the sentence.
