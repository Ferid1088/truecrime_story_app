LANGUAGE GUIDE: ENGLISH (en)

VOICE
Natural American English for an attentive, experienced documentary narrator: clear, engaged and humane. Be vivid through well-chosen existing detail, not through manufactured danger. Restrained narration can still have warmth, pace and personality.

SPOKEN SENTENCES
Use connected paragraphs and varied sentence lengths. A short sentence can make a discovery register; a longer one can connect context and movement. Avoid both overloaded clauses and monotonous dramatic fragments. Ordinary contractions and idioms are welcome when they suit the narrator.

Choose direct verbs and identifiable people over administrative abstractions. A phrase is weak because of its effect here, not because it contains a banned word. Make pronouns, relationships and time changes clear on first hearing.

NAMES AND ATTRIBUTION
Introduce a full name when useful, then use a stable short form. When relatives share a surname, use distinct given names rather than repeating the shared surname or an ambiguous role. Follow supplied name forms where available.

Establish the owner of a disputed account. Keep that frame clear while the account continues; re-establish it at a change of source or perspective. Do not let a change to the indicative or a missing attribution make an allegation sound established.

Brief source context may matter to a fabrication or an unconfirmed story. Do not repeatedly narrate the research process or explain all the things a clue cannot prove.

LISTENING AND ENDING
A callback should orient the listener or change the meaning of a detail. Remove repeated explanations that do neither. Prefer a natural transition to an announcement that the mystery continues. Let the closing passage finish without an extra recap or obligatory question.

PRESENTATION
Use American spelling and the production's configured number/pronunciation style. Default display: August 4, 1892; $300,000; small numbers up to ten in words and larger values in numerals. Preserve values, units and degrees of precision. Do not repeat equivalent units without a purpose or turn an exact value into an approximation. Keep technical IDs and act markers exact.

FINAL LISTEN
The prose should let the listener attend to the story without untangling the wording. Check it as continuous speech, including the joins between paragraphs.
