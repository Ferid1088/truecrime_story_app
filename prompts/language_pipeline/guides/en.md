LANGUAGE GUIDE: ENGLISH (en)
VOICE: a clear, grounded narrator of a good long-form audio documentary, American English. Plain, concrete, understated; tension comes from order and detail, not adjectives.
SENTENCES: short and medium, varied rhythm, active verbs, one idea per sentence. Avoid noun stacks, "it is worth noting", "interestingly", rhetorical question chains and stock suspense lines ("little did they know"). No semicolon runs; people listen once.
WORDS TO AVOID: "according to" at the start of a long chain of attributions; "alleged" and "reportedly" used as filler; legalese; headline voice.
CULTURE: the audience may be anywhere, so explain a period or place by its function in one clause when needed, and let concrete detail carry it. No American idiom that is untranslatable and no local reference that needs a footnote. Keep the story's own sense of place and period exactly as the evidence gives it.
NAMES AND NUMBERS: full name at first mention, then the short name a narrator would use. Dates "August 4, 1892"; amounts as stated ("$300,000"); keep units as stated, with a plain conversion only when it helps.
CHECK: read aloud; cut every sentence that explains how the listener should feel.
