#! Purpose: the rules for the title of every cover (the long film and each short) written as a hook; appended to cover_planner.md, followed by the guide of the language (hooks/<language>.md).
HOOK RULES for the titles. The title is the only thing a stranger reads on the cover in the first second. He knows nothing about the case, the channel or the genre. The title alone must make him stop.

WHAT A HOOK IS
- ONE short sentence or noun phrase of the number of words in "limits.title_words" with a twist inside it: two things that do not fit together, a thing that did what it should not, a time or a place that carries dread.
- The strongest pattern is a noun and a relative clause with an irony in it: "the killer who was never meant to be found", "a love that demanded a victim", "the day death found life", "a silence louder than any scream".
- The tone is dark: night, silence, home, trust, time, death, fear. It is never graphic: no blood, wound, weapon or body is named.
- The reader must feel at once that this is a real crime and want to know how it came to this. A mood title ("The Silence", "Dark Waters") fails: it says nothing and asks nothing.

WHAT A HOOK NEVER DOES
1. It never spoils. It never says or hints WHO did it, that someone lied, that something was staged or covered up, that the culprit was close to the victim, a motive, a verdict or any answer of the film (see "answer_acts"). Test it: if a viewer who knows the title and not the film could guess a fact of the plot from it, it fails.
2. It names no person, place or date. A name gives a stranger nothing. The only number allowed is one that is part of the idea ("two places, one night").
3. It is true. It says only what the film's facts bear out; the twist is in the wording, never in an invented fact. Nobody "heard a scream", "found a note" or "saw a stranger" unless the film's facts say so. What a person said is never stated as a fact.
4. It is no clickbait: no "you will not believe", no "the truth about", no question mark piled on a question mark, no all-capital shouting, no greeting, no channel name.
5. It is one idea. No list, no two sentences, no colon with a subtitle.
6. Every cover has its own title; the short covers' titles differ from the film's and each reflects its own clip.

7. It reads at a glance on a phone: the cover sets the title as large as the picture allows and sends back a title that is too long to read at a useful size. Prefer the shortest wording that keeps the twist.

HOW TO WRITE IT IN THE LANGUAGE OF THE INPUT
Write it as a native speaker would say it on a poster, not as a translation of an English phrase. If a pattern does not work idiomatically in the language, change the pattern, keep the twist. Do not copy an example; they show the pattern, not the words.
